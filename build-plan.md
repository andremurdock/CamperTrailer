# 5x10 Cargo Camper Build Plan & Manual

Oct 2026 · Andre

## Overview

The existing 5x10 single-axle cargo trailer becomes a two-person camper: a convertible bed/sofa/desk up front, a bolt-in steel false wall with a door and window at the rear, and the ramp lowered as a 5x5 ft deck under a rear awning. The ramp still works for hauling once the false wall is unbolted. Target finished weight is roughly 2,000-2,500 lb with water, the battery and the tongue power box (estimate; confirm on a scale).

### Design decisions

- Full tri-fold mattress on removable plywood panels held by E-track 2x4 beam sockets. One panel moves up to become the desk.
- Three horizontal E-track rows on both walls, plus short vertical strips near the sofa for an adjustable desk height.
- Bolt-in false wall made from the same steel tube as the trailer frame, removable for big loads. It holds the door, a window and the heater duct port.
- Windows: two per side, one high in the front wall with a removable rock guard, and one in the false wall. One side window is sized as an emergency exit. Doors, windows and the skylight are bought used (eBay, RV salvage, Marketplace) to save money.
- Roof, front to back: roof fan over the head of the bed, 12V rooftop AC/heater in the middle over the axle, 14 x 22 in skylight over the entry.
- Budget power: a 200Ah lithium battery charged by a 200W folding solar panel, the Champion 2500 generator, or campsite power through an 80A converter/charger. Everything that runs at night is 12V; the 120V outlets and water heater work only while the generator or campsite power is on.
- A small vented battery box on the tongue. The generator and solar panel ride in the truck bed.
- Wash corner just inside the false wall: an open sink stand with a 12V pump, water jugs and a small 120V water heater, and a portable cassette toilet that slides out underneath.
- A portable diesel heater runs outside on or near the deck and ducts warm air in through the false wall.
- Cooking is outside on a propane camp stove. No propane or fuel-burning appliances inside.

Tow vehicle: 2017 Ram 1500 5.7L with a 4 in lift, 37 in tires and 3.92 gears. Plan on a raised ball mount so the trailer tows level.

## Tools, safety, and shop setup

Do all cutting, grinding and welding before any foam goes in. Polyiso and wiring catch sparks easily.

### Tools

- Drill/driver, impact driver, step bits, cobalt bits for steel
- Rivet nut tool and pop rivet gun
- Jigsaw with fine metal blades, or a nibbler, for wall cut-outs
- Angle grinder with cut-off and flap discs
- MIG welder, or a welding shop for the false wall frame
- Circular saw or track saw for plywood
- Oscillating multi-tool, utility knife, caulk gun, heat gun
- Level, square, tape, chalk line, magnet to find steel posts
- Multimeter, wire stripper, ratcheting crimper, heat-shrink
- Clamps, sawhorses, jack stands, wheel chocks

### Safety

- Safety glasses, hearing protection and gloves for all metal work; a respirator when cutting foam or grinding.
- Keep a fire extinguisher within reach when grinding or welding, and check for smoldering spots 30 minutes after.
- Chock the wheels and support the tongue and rear corners on jack stands before working inside.
- Keep shore power unplugged and the battery disconnected while wiring.
- If you are unsure about the 120 V side, have an electrician check it before you plug in.

### Shop setup

- Level, covered spot with 120 V power and good light.
- Park so you can open the ramp fully and walk around all sides.
- Keep a build log: photos of every wall before it is closed, and the measurements in Phase 1.

## Build sequence

The order follows four rules: holes and metal first, wires before walls close, walls before E-track, furniture last. Expect roughly 10-14 weekends for a handy builder (estimate).

| 3 | Electrical rough-in | 1-2 days | Every wire run, labeled, photographed |

## Phase 1: Strip, inspect, measure

The goal is a clean shell, a list of problems to fix, and real measurements to replace every estimate in this plan.

1. Empty and wash the trailer. Run a hose over the roof seams, corners and ramp seal while someone watches inside. Mark every leak with tape.
2. Inspect the frame, axle, springs, hangers and tongue for rust or cracks. Check tire date codes, bearings, lights and wiring.
3. Check whether the trailer has brakes. Note Georgia's brake requirement for your finished weight.
4. Remove the interior wall plywood. Number each sheet on the back so it can go back in the same spot.
5. Fill in the measurement table below.
6. Mark the layout on the floor with painter's tape: the 75 in bed zone, the false wall line 4-5 in inside the ramp seal, the sink and toilet corner, the roof layout and the window positions.
7. Weigh the empty trailer at a CAT scale, total and tongue, as a baseline.

## Phase 2: Cut-ins and metalwork

Every hole, weld and grind happens in this phase, while the walls are still bare steel and skin. Buy the windows, roof fan, door, skylight and rooftop AC first so you cut to their exact rough openings.

### Buying used doors and windows

- Search eBay, RV salvage yards around Atlanta and Facebook Marketplace. Local pickup avoids $100+ shipping on a door.
- Every door and window clamps to a wall-thickness range. Yours lands around 1.25-1.5 in, so look for "1-1/2 in wall" or adjustable clamp rings.
- Make sure the trim ring, screen, latch and keys are included, and check for cracked frames or cloudy acrylic.
- The door should be under 60 in tall and hinged on the right for the curbside.
- Buy everything first, then cut each opening to the part you actually have.

### Side windows (4)

1. Check each window's rough opening size and the wall-thickness range its clamp ring fits. Your finished wall will be skin + 1 in foam + the reinstalled plywood.
2. Place each window between wall posts, in the band between the middle and high E-track rows (about 31-50 in off the floor). Front pair beside the bed, rear pair by the entry.
3. Size one window to work as an emergency exit and check its rated clear opening.
4. Mark the opening from inside, drill the corners, confirm the position from outside, then cut with a fine metal jigsaw blade or nibbler.
5. Frame each opening with tube or a wood buck tied into the posts so the clamp ring has something solid to grab.
6. Deburr and prime the cut edges. Tape plastic over the holes until Phase 4. Windows go in after the wall panels so the clamp ring matches the finished thickness.

### Front window

1. Center it high on the front wall, about 38-52 in off the floor, between the front wall posts. That clears the tongue box outside and the sofa back inside.
2. Choose acrylic or polycarbonate glazing, since the truck's 37 in tires throw rocks.
3. Drill mounting points for a removable rock guard that bolts on for travel.

### Roof fan

1. Center the 14 x 14 in opening over the bed, between two roof bows.
2. Frame the opening, then install the fan flange on butyl tape and cover the screws and edges with self-leveling lap sealant. This is the most leak-prone hole on the trailer.

### Roof layout: fan, AC, skylight

1. Lay out the roof front to back: roof fan centered \~24 in from the front wall, AC opening (14 x 14 in) centered \~62 in back, skylight (\~14 x 22 in) centered \~98 in back. Check each against the roof bow spacing first; the skylight may need one bow cut and headered.
2. Cut and frame the fan and skylight openings. Set each on butyl tape and cover every screw with lap sealant.
3. Build a cross-frame between two roof bows under the AC opening to carry the 45 lb unit, then cut the opening.
4. Once the AC is on, measure the new total height and post it in the truck.

### False wall frame

1. Build the frame from the same tube as the trailer posts: a perimeter frame, a door opening about 24 in wide and under 60 in tall, a window opening about 24 x 18 in with its bottom above the 34 in sink counter, a \~3 in heater duct port low in the center section, and a header over each.
2. Weld bolt-on tabs to the frame where it meets the wall posts, the floor crossmember and the nearest roof bow. Fit rivet nuts in the trailer side.
3. Set it 4-5 in inside the ramp seal. Dry-fit it, bolt it in, and close the ramp to confirm clearance for the door handle and window frame.
4. Prime and paint it, then leave it bolted in for the rest of the build.

### Other holes

- Battery box mounting holes on the tongue frame.
- Pass-through in the front wall for the battery and AC power cables, with a grommet.
- Shore power inlet, usually low on the roadside wall near the front.
- Weatherproof solar port on the side wall near the front.

Finish with a full hose test before moving on.

## Phase 3: Electrical rough-in

Run every wire now, while the walls are open. Nothing gets connected or energized until Phase 8.

### Two systems

- **12 V (battery):** lights, roof fan, USB and 12V sockets, water pump, the rooftop AC/heater and the outside 12V outlet for the diesel heater. Fed from the 200Ah lithium battery through a fuse block; the AC gets its own heavy cable, 100A fuse and disconnect. This side runs all night.
- **120 V (generator or campsite only):** outlets, the converter/charger and the water heater, fed from the 30A inlet through a breaker panel. There is no inverter, so these are off once the generator stops. Add a small inverter later only if something 120V must run at night.

### Runs to make

| Run | From | To | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Battery cables | Battery box | Fuse block inside front wall | Short, heavy gauge, fused at the battery |
| Rooftop AC | Battery box | Middle of roof | \~12-15 ft; heavy cable, 100A fuse, disconnect switch |
| Ceiling lights | Fuse block | 2-3 spots on the ceiling | One over the bed, one over the entry |
| Roof fan | Fuse block | Front of roof | Own fused circuit |
| Water pump | Fuse block | Sink stand |  |
| USB and 12V sockets | Fuse block | Head of bed and desk area | Laptops charge with a USB-C or 12V charger |
| Outside 12V outlet | Fuse block | False wall | Plug-in connector so the wall stays removable |
| Solar | Side-wall solar port | Charge controller, then battery | Heavy wire, sealed entry |
| Generator/campsite | 30A inlet | 120V breaker panel | 30 A cable |
| Converter/charger | Breaker panel | Battery | Mount inside near the panel; heavy output cable to the battery |
| Outlets | Breaker panel | Desk area and entry | GFCI protected; live on generator or campsite power |
| Water heater | Breaker panel | Sink stand | Own circuit |

### Rules

1. Use stranded copper wire sized for each circuit's current and length. Use proper 120 V cable on the shore side.
2. Protect every pass-through in steel with a grommet or bushing.
3. Label both ends of every wire.
4. Leave a service loop at each device.
5. Photograph every wall from straight on with a tape measure in frame before closing it.
6. If you are unsure about the 120 V side, have an electrician review it before Phase 8.

## Phase 4: Insulation, floor, walls, ceiling

In a space this small, insulation matters more than the size of the AC or heater. Aim for no gaps and no bare steel facing the inside.

### Floor

1. If you can reach the underside, fit rigid foam or closed-cell spray foam between the floor crossmembers. This keeps all your interior height.
2. Lay washable vinyl or rubber flooring on top. Run it to the walls so E-track and the false wall sit on it.

### Walls

1. Cut 1 in polyiso to fit snugly between the posts, working around the wires. Fill gaps with low-expansion can foam and tape the seams.
2. Cover the inside face of each steel post with foil tape or a thin foam strip to cut the cold bridge.
3. Screw 3/4 in plywood backer strips, about 4-6 in tall, across the posts at each E-track row height: \~10 in, \~30-31 in and \~52 in.
4. Reinstall the numbered wall plywood, fitting it between or over the backer strips. Cut openings for windows, outlets and switches.

### Ceiling

1. Fit foam between the roof bows. Hold it with a few dabs of foam-safe adhesive.
2. Screw a thin ceiling panel (1/8-1/4 in) to the bows. Cut the fan and light openings.

### Front wall

Insulate it the same way, leaving the duct and battery-cable pass-throughs open and sealed with grommets.

### Close up openings

1. Install the side windows, the front window and the false-wall window on butyl tape, with clamp rings matched to the finished wall thickness.
2. Trim the roof fan and skylight inside.
3. Hose test again.

## Phase 5: E-track

E-track is what makes the bed, desk and shelves modular, so the rows on both walls must match in height to within about 1/8 in.

| Row | Height off floor | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | \~10 in | Bed beams, cargo tie-downs |
| Middle | \~30-31 in | Desk/counter beams, sofa-back strap |
| High | \~52 in | Shelves, nets, cabinet fittings |
| Vertical strips | Near the sofa zone | Fine-tuning desk height |

1. Measure each row height from the floor at both ends of each wall. Use the floor as the reference, not a bubble level, since the trailer may not sit level.
2. Snap a chalk line and fasten the track into the backer strips. Where the track crosses a steel post, add a self-tapping screw into the post.
3. Break the rows cleanly at windows, the AC and the door. Leave about 1 in of clearance.
4. Fit short vertical strips on both walls beside the sofa zone, mirrored exactly.
5. Clip in two 2x4 beam sockets at matching heights on opposite walls. Cut a 2x4 to the measured width minus about 1/4 in and test-fit it.
6. Plan four bed beams on the low row: one at each panel edge, at 0, 24, 48 and 72 in from the front wall.
7. Don't overtighten screws into plywood. Use one size up if a screw spins.

## Phase 6: False wall skin, door, window, install

The frame was built and dry-fit in Phase 2. Now it becomes a finished, sealed wall that still unbolts in about 30 minutes.

### Skin and insulate

1. Unbolt the frame and lay it flat on sawhorses.
2. Rivet or bond a thin aluminum or FRP skin to the deck side. Cut the door and window openings after the skin is on.
3. Fit 3/4-1 in polyiso between the tubes.
4. Attach the interior panel. Finished thickness should land around 1.25-1.5 in.

### Door and window

1. Confirm the door and window clamp-ring ranges match the finished thickness.
2. Set each on butyl tape, clamp it, and adjust the latch and strike until the door closes with light pressure.
3. Use a latch that opens from inside without a key.

### Install

1. Bolt the wall in through the tabs into the rivet nuts.
2. Seal the perimeter with a compressible EPDM gasket rather than permanent sealant, so the wall can come out.
3. Add a drip edge above the door and window, since this wall gets rained on when the ramp is down.
4. Close the ramp and confirm it clears the door handle and window frame, then open and close it several times.

### Removal for hauling

1. Unbolt all tabs and lift the wall out with a helper.
2. Store it flat, door side up.
3. Reinstall with the same gasket and check the seal with a hose.

## Phase 7: Climate and safety

The rooftop AC handles cooling and mild heat; the portable diesel heater covers cold nights. The heater burns fuel, so it always runs outside, and the CO detector is not optional.

### Tongue battery box

1. Bolt a small vented, lockable battery box to the tongue for the 200Ah battery (about 21 x 8 x 8.5 in, 46 lb).
2. Keep the converter/charger inside the camper near the breaker panel, not in the box.
3. Carry the generator and the folding solar panel in the truck bed, chained and locked.
4. Weigh the tongue again once the battery is in.
5. The old Mopar H7 starting battery can run the tongue jack or stay as a spare. It is not for house power.

### Portable diesel heater

1. Set the heater on the ground just off the roadside corner of the deck, or in that corner of the deck on a heat shield. Point the exhaust away from the chairs and out from under the awning.
2. Push its warm-air hose into the duct port in the false wall, and cap the port when not in use.
3. Plug it into the outside 12V outlet on the false wall.
4. Refuel over a spill tray.

### Rooftop AC/heater

1. Lift the 45 lb unit onto the cross-framed opening in the middle of the roof and bolt it through with its gasket. Seal around the base.
2. Mount the indoor panel on the ceiling.
3. Run the heavy 12V cable from the battery box through the 100A fuse and disconnect switch.
4. On the generator, the 80A converter/charger runs the AC by charging the battery. It keeps up with the AC's 21-58A draw.
5. Use the AC's built-in heater only to take the chill off while the generator runs. It drains the battery fast.

### Roof fan

Connect it to its fused 12 V circuit. Use exhaust mode with a window cracked for airflow.

### Safety

- Smoke + CO detector near the head of the bed.
- Fire extinguisher by the door.
- No propane or butane appliances inside.

### Running on the generator

- Before 9 pm: run the generator for the AC and to top off the battery. Refilling half of 200Ah takes about 1-1.5 hours.
- After 9 pm: AC on battery in sleep or eco mode with a timer. Expect 3-5 hours on hot nights and more on cool ones, then switch to the roof fan.
- Set the AC's low-voltage cutoff to save enough battery for lights, the fan and the diesel heater until morning.
- The Champion's outlets are 20 A household plugs, so connect the camper's 30 A cord with an adapter.
- Park it well away from the camper, exhaust pointed away from the door, windows and awning. Never run it on the deck, under the awning or in the truck bed with the cover closed.
- Chain and lock it.

## Phase 8: Electrical finish and solar

Connect, then test each circuit one at a time before turning everything on together.

### 12 V side

1. Set the Redodo 200Ah battery in the tongue battery box and fit the main fuse close to the positive terminal.
2. Connect the battery cables to the fuse block inside the front wall.
3. Connect lights, switches, USB and 12V sockets, roof fan, water pump and the outside 12V outlet, each on its own fuse.
4. Connect the rooftop AC through its own 100A fuse and disconnect switch.
5. Mount the solar charge controller near the fuse block and wire it to the battery.

### 120 V side

1. Connect the 30A inlet to the breaker panel.
2. Mount the PD89RR converter/charger near the panel, set it to LiFePO4, and run its output to the battery.
3. Wire the GFCI outlets and the water heater circuit.
4. Keep the 30A-to-20A adapter for the generator; buy a 50A-to-30A adapter only if you hit a 50A-only site.

### Solar

Use one 200W folding panel, set out in the sun like the Starlink dish while the trailer sits in shade. Run a 30-50 ft heavy extension cable to the solar port on the side wall, then to the charge controller. Solar tops up the lights, fan and pump but will not keep up with the AC.

### Test

1. With a multimeter, check polarity and voltage at every 12 V device before connecting it.
2. Press the test button on each GFCI.
3. Plug in the generator and confirm the converter/charger shows it is charging.
4. Run the AC on the generator, then on battery alone, and note the amps.

## Phase 9: Bed, sofa, desk, shelves, sink and toilet

Three plywood panels and four 2x4 beams make the bed at night and the sofa plus desk by day. Nothing is fixed, so the whole floor clears for hauling.

### Make the panels

1. Cut three panels about 57 x 24 in from two 4x8 sheets of 1/2-5/8 in plywood (two panels per sheet). Trim the width to your measured interior width minus about 1/4 in.
2. Round the corners, sand the edges, and seal all faces.
3. Screw a cleat under each panel's edges so it can't slide off the beams.
4. Laminate or skin one panel with a heat-resistant, wipeable top. That's the desk and counter.

### Night: bed

1. Drop four beams into the low-row sockets at 0, 24, 48 and 72 in from the front wall.
2. Lay the three panels across them, then the ventilated underlay, then the tri-fold.
3. Sleep height is about 16 in, with about 15 in of headroom sitting up.

### Day: sofa and desk

1. Fold the front mattress section up against the front wall as the sofa back. Strap it to the middle row.
2. Leave the front panel on the low beams as the seat base. The seat lands about 22 in high.
3. Move the desk panel onto two beams in the middle row or the vertical strips, at about 30-32 in.
4. Strap the third panel upright against a side wall.
5. Sit on the sofa and adjust the desk height until your forearms rest flat. Note the final height here.

### Shelves

- Clip shelves onto the high row with single-stud fittings and E-track shelf brackets.
- Keep them 8-10 in deep over the bed, deeper over the entry.
- Add a front lip, rail or cargo net so nothing falls out on the road.

### Wash corner

1. Build an open sink stand that clips onto the E-track along the roadside wall, just inside the false wall: a 34 in counter with a bar sink on top and open shelves below.
2. Put the 12V pump, the fresh and gray water jugs and the \~2.5 gal 120V water heater on the shelves, with quick-connect fittings so the stand comes out for hauling.
3. Mount the portable cassette toilet on a sliding tray at floor level under the stand, on slides rated for 100+ lb, with a latch. It pulls out toward the curbside, which leaves knee room across the trailer.
4. Strap everything down or fit a bar across the front for travel, since there are no doors.
5. Heat water during the evening generator run; the tank stays hot for hours.

### Lock-down for travel

Every panel, beam, shelf and the toilet is strapped or latched before the ramp closes.

## Phase 10: Ramp deck and rear awning

The 5x5 ft ramp becomes a level porch for two chairs, shaded by the rear awning.

### Deck

1. Mount two adjustable legs or screw jacks under the ramp's outer corners. They carry the deck's weight so the ramp hinges and spring cables don't.
2. Level the deck with the trailer floor.
3. Lay interlocking deck tiles or an outdoor rug over the ramp ridges for a flat, grippy surface.
4. Fit a removable railing in sockets along the sides and outer edge, plus a step if the deck sits high.
5. Keep about 2 ft clear in front of the false-wall door for its outward swing. The two chairs go in the outer 3 ft.

### Rear awning

1. Mount the keder rail on the roof's rear edge or rear cap, above where the ramp seals, so the ramp still closes tight.
2. Size the awning about 6 ft wide with 7-8 ft of projection, so it covers the whole deck and a little past it.
3. Set the poles on the ground just past the deck edge, not on the deck.
4. Angle the awning slightly down and away for runoff. Stake and guy-line both outer corners.
5. Close the ramp with the rail installed to confirm clearance.

### Outdoor kitchen

- Cook on a propane camp stove on a folding table at the deck or campsite, out from under the awning fabric.
- Use a separate propane tank from the generator's.

## Phase 11 (optional): Off-road running gear

This phase is independent of the interior and is easiest before heavy items go in. It suits forest roads and rough campgrounds, not rock crawling.

1. **Clearance:** flip the leaf springs over the axle (a spring-over conversion) or fit a lift axle and hanger kit. Expect roughly 3-4 in of extra ground clearance.
2. **Tires:** fit all-terrain trailer tires rated well above the loaded axle weight, plus a matching spare. Check the bolt pattern.
3. **Fender clearance:** cycle the suspension by hand and check that the larger tires clear the fenders and body.
4. **Bearings and brakes:** repack or replace the bearings. Add electric brakes if the finished weight calls for them.
5. **Hitch height:** measure the coupler height after the lift. Match it with a raised ball mount on the Ram (4 in lift, 37 in tires) so the trailer tows level.
6. **Re-weigh** total and tongue weight.
